# Cat Run product requirements

Version 0.3.0 · Updated October 17, 2026

Cat Run is a small native macOS utility for keeping a Mac awake for a chosen duration. It is created by Nikhil Sharma with assistance from OpenAI’s ChatGPT models, inspired by Caffeine by Dominic Rodemer, and published as open source under the MIT licence.

## Timer and completion

- Offer durations from 15 minutes to 6 hours in 15-minute increments. Use two-digit hour and minute values, omitting hours below one hour. Save the last three distinct successfully started durations for quick activation; show “No timers used yet.” when history is empty.
- Keep the Mac awake while a timer runs. Keeping the display awake is optional and can be changed only while inactive. Keep the Mac-awake checkbox checked and locked, with the hint “Keeps your Mac awake whenever you start a timer.”
- Show remaining time while active. Allow 15-minute extensions up to a total session duration of six hours. Keep Start and Stop in the same position and at the same size.
- For every terminal outcome, show **“Timer stopped · Reason”**, with a compact specific reason beside the common title. Natural completion uses “Time elapsed”; manual stop uses “Stopped by you”. Show a known stop date, time, and timezone below using the Mac’s current regional conventions and local timezone, including its applicable daylight-saving offset. Show hours and minutes without seconds.
- Capture the completion instant once. A delayed callback must use the timer’s deadline rather than the later time the window is opened or refreshed. Region or timezone changes may reformat that same instant. Wall-clock settings must not alter the countdown duration.
- Keep one encrypted record for the most recent session outcome, including natural completion, and restore its notice after relaunch. This main-screen record is separate from the seven-day activity history. A successfully started timer replaces that outcome. Failed activation displays the applicable failure without adding a recent duration. A failure after writing the new session marker may replace the saved outcome with that failed attempt.
- Cover manual stop, time elapsed, battery below 15%, unavailable battery reading, unavailable timer clock, failed keep-awake request, sleep, app quit, unresponsive app, user switch, system-session ending, and session-storage failure. Preserve the distinction between these reasons. Do not send session notifications.
- Closing the window leaves a timer running. Quitting, system sleep, user switching, and observed logout/shutdown end it. Relaunch inactive with saved duration, screen-awake preference, and recent durations; never restore an active timer.

## Interruption and outcome handling

- Write a session marker before acquiring sleep-prevention assertions. Persist the final reason and observed time after releasing them. A new process that finds an unfinished marker records **“Timer stopped · Unexpected interruption”** and **“Cause and stop time unknown.”** Record the detection instant separately in history; never treat reopening time as the stop time or infer successful completion from elapsed time. Do not claim to distinguish an app crash, force quit, Mac crash, power loss, or other abrupt failure without evidence.
- For an observed macOS system-ending notification, use the combined reason “Logout, restart or shutdown”; do not invent which occurred. A later cancellation does not restart a stopped timer.
- Store a single bounded encrypted record with atomic writes in the app's local container. Migrate valid older plaintext records in place with no plaintext backup. Reject malformed records, unexpected filesystem objects, and oversized input. Preserve authenticated malformed records separately and show that prior status is unavailable; authentication or missing-key failures must not silently replace the file or key. A failure to create the new session record prevents activation.
- Limit corrupt-record copies to three newest files and seven days. Delete expired copies before requesting their key; encrypt retained bounded legacy copies in place without resetting their retention age. Handle only app-owned UUID-named regular copies, never symlinks or unrelated files. Preserve unavailable recent data without making plaintext duplicates and report maintenance failure. The separate encrypted last-session notice may outlive the activity log retention window.
- Cancel on system sleep. Also reconcile an active timer on wake and compare continuous time with awake time to detect sleep when a notification is missed. When sleep is discovered later, keep the stop instant unknown and explicitly label its detection date in the notice and activity entry. Display sleep alone does not imply system sleep. Do not resume after waking, returning to the user session, or cancelling a logout/shutdown that has already stopped Cat Run.
- Keep stop processing serialized and tied to the current session identifier. An older callback must not stop, extend, resume, or overwrite a later session. Preserve the first recorded terminal outcome.
- Evaluate evidence of sleep before declaring completion. Without that evidence, a deadline already reached takes precedence over a low-battery reading first observed afterward. A low reading detected before the deadline stops the timer for low battery. Date/time text for safety stops is the observed stop time; it cannot establish the exact instant at which charge crossed a threshold.

## Battery protection and power use

- Refuse to start below 15% battery and stop an active timer below 15%, including while plugged in. Exactly 15% is allowed. If the Mac has a battery but its charge cannot be verified, block or stop the timer.
- Warn before starting or extending only when a usable forecast estimates ending charge below 15%. An unavailable runtime is unknown, not evidence of low charge: it must not trigger a warning, block activation with a valid charge reading, or fabricate an estimate. This corrects the earlier warning seen at 90% after unplugging.
- Use the current macOS internal-battery runtime only after at least 30 seconds of stable on-battery observations. Discard forecast confidence on wake, AC connection, missing/invalid charge or runtime, clock regression, a gap over 120 seconds, or a large runtime change. The stability check allows a change of up to five minutes or 25% of the previous runtime, whichever is larger, accounting for elapsed time. Return only the current estimate; retain no substitute for missing runtime. Actual percentage safety checks apply immediately throughout settling.
- While active, show a low projection in the existing notice area as it becomes available, including after unplugging. Do not open a new warning dialog mid-session. Keep actual-charge protection in force regardless of whether a forecast exists.
- Use macOS power-source notifications and refresh on app launch, activation, and wake. While active, an independent serial safety queue obtains current charge every 10 seconds, allowing up to one second of timer tolerance. It performs no battery polling while idle.
- Renew native sleep-prevention assertions with an operating-system timeout of at most 45 seconds, shortened to the remaining session time. Check a main-thread heartbeat with a 30-second timeout; when the interface stalls, the safety queue releases assertions and records the stop without waiting for it. If the safety queue stalls too, its last assertion retains its OS timeout. Do not silently reacquire an expired lease.
- Release assertions on completion, cancellation, safety stop, or quit. macOS may override them for explicit sleep, lid closure, shutdown, or system safety. OS scheduling and battery reports limit response timing; the guard cannot promise an exact battery floor or reliable execution during a kernel/system failure.

## Interface and menu bar

- Use a black background with peach `#FBC193` text and outlines. Keep ordinary labels equally bright. Deliberately fade the disabled duration slider and its minus/plus symbols and borders while active, with no pressed feedback; do not make inactive controls look actionable. The separate +15m action remains visibly enabled when extension is allowed. Use Verdana for controls and a script wordmark, with the same sitting cat as the idle menu-bar icon.
- Align the wordmark, cat, and navigation button at the bottom. Center Settings and Back labels within their buttons. Use a rounded duration slider with a centered thumb, without a duplicate duration dropdown. Reserve enough clearance at both endpoints for the complete circular thumb, outline, and shadow in active and inactive states.
- Preserve the full raster edges of lettering and artwork, including antialiasing outside ideal glyph bounds. Check main, active, Settings, warning, and hover states at both window limits; include long history values, all login statuses, and localized outcome dates throughout the year. Resizing must not crop text or overlap fixed controls.
- Keep the main and Settings screens free of scrollbars. The Activity page alone uses a vertical scrollbar, with no horizontal scrolling. The minimum content area is 420 × 400 points. Choose the default size for the display; preserve the complete window’s aspect ratio when resizing, up to 25% above its default width and height while fitting the usable display. Hide and disable the green zoom control.
- Show a static cat in the menu bar while inactive. While active, choose from [20 actions](CAT-ACTIONS.md) every five seconds without immediately repeating an action; show two-digit hours above minutes beside the cat. Left-click opens the app. Right-click offers only Open and Quit.
- Reduce animation work in Low Power Mode and suppress motion for Reduce Motion, sleeping displays, or serious thermal pressure. Cache artwork and bound the combined cat/countdown cache.

## Activity history

- Add **View logs** in Settings. Open an Activity page inside the existing window without changing its size or aspect ratio. **Back** returns to Settings; another Back returns to the main screen. Logs remain available while a timer runs.
- Show concise entries with the most recently recorded activity first. Preserve recording order when the wall clock moves; do not reorder the sequence of actions by their displayed timestamp. Start each local calendar day with a clear date-only separator and thin rule, using regional formatting. Never label headings Today or Yesterday. Keep the current date heading visible while scrolling; repeat a date heading if a clock correction changes the day sequence.
- Each row shows local hours, minutes, seconds, and timezone plus one short description. Seconds aid auditing and do not change the hours/minutes-only timer or stop notice. Resize rows to fit their text at both window limits, with a visible vertical scrollbar and no overlap.
- Record app opening/normal exit/detected unrecorded exit; timer start, extension, stop and reason; warnings, warning choices, blocked actions and failures; observed sleep/wake and user-session changes; power connection changes; and duration, keep-screen-awake, and login-setting changes. Do not record countdown ticks, regular battery checks, or animation frames.
- Show “No activity logged yet.” for an empty history. Report unavailable or unverifiable logs clearly. Do not silently lose logging and then claim a complete history: mark resumed recording when some entries may be missing. A crash, failed write, or interrupted shutdown can prevent a final entry; missing exit/stop causes and times remain unknown.
- Retain a rolling seven days, capped at 4,000 entries and a bounded encrypted file size. Delete expired entries on reads/writes and hourly maintenance while running; after a long absence, apply retention on the next launch. Moving the clock backward must not restore already expired events. Retry delivery of the same event must not duplicate it.
- Encrypt both activity history and the last-session marker using AES-256-GCM with distinct authenticated contexts. Store the 256-bit key in macOS Keychain using this app's access controls, never beside the data or in public source. Do not generate a replacement key when encrypted data already exists. Missing keys, authentication failures, and storage failures must never fall back to plaintext.
- Run activity-log encryption and file work on a separate utility queue. A logging failure must not prevent releasing power assertions; limit how long normal quit waits for pending log writes. Logging does not add networking, telemetry, accounts, a privileged helper, or new entitlements. Collect app-owned event descriptions without personal details, user file paths, or device identifiers.

## Settings and distribution

- Allow launch at login to be changed only while inactive, using macOS’s login-item service. Report registration failures and approval requirements accurately.
- About must show the creator, OpenAI ChatGPT assistance, Caffeine attribution, version, update date, and MIT licence.
- Support macOS 13 or later using native Swift and macOS frameworks. Keep App Sandbox and hardened runtime enabled with no permissions beyond those needed. Do not add telemetry, network access, accounts, ads, notification permission, or a privileged helper.
- Keep personal contact details, preferences, caches, signing material, and generated binaries out of the public source repository. Publish source under MIT. Public binary distribution requires separate signing, notarization, and clean-machine validation.

## Stop notice and activity acceptance criteria

1. Every terminal timer outcome uses the same “Timer stopped” title with its concise reason. Natural completion says “Time elapsed”; manual, battery, sleep, quit, user-switch, system-ending, and safety outcomes stay distinct.
2. A known stop instant appears on the second line in local date/time/timezone format. Refreshing, reopening, or relaunching does not change it. A late awake callback still reports the deadline, and an extension uses its extended deadline.
3. Unknown interruptions show unknown cause and stop time; logs timestamp detection explicitly. A missed sleep notification shows the detected date without claiming it was the actual stop instant. App/system crashes and force quits are never distinguished by guesswork.
4. Timezone conversion handles date rollover and daylight-saving changes; regional formatting respects the local hour cycle. Countdown duration remains monotonic. A successful new timer replaces the old main-screen outcome; failed starts do not fabricate completion.
5. Notices and all fixed controls fit at minimum and maximum window sizes. Disabled minus/plus controls and slider look inactive, while allowed extensions remain actionable.
6. Settings → View logs preserves window size, shows readable date-only groups, starts at the newest recorded entry, and Back returns to Settings. Long log text wraps without clipping; only this page scrolls.
7. Verify seven-day expiry, the 4,000-entry cap, event deduplication, clock correction, empty/error states, key loss, tampering, migration, quarantine retention, and interrupted writes with automated tests. Physical sleep, shutdown, crash, and Keychain integration checks remain separate; report only checks actually performed in VALIDATION.md.

## Scenario acceptance matrix

The following is the expected policy for the 22 reviewed scenarios. “Automated” identifies policy-test scope using controlled clocks, battery values, and power-service doubles; it does not itself claim that those tests passed in this release or that the physical action was performed on a Mac. “Manual” identifies integration checks still needed unless explicitly recorded in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).

| # | Scenario during an active timer | Expected result | Verification coverage and remaining checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close a laptop lid in ordinary use | If macOS sleeps, cancel; retain the sleep reason and remain inactive on wake. | Automated sleep-gap and journal tests; manual lid/wake check. |
| 2 | Close the lid with an external monitor | Continue if macOS stays awake in its supported closed-lid configuration; otherwise use the sleep-stop behavior. | Automated no-sleep/sleep clock paths; manual external-display setup. |
| 3 | Choose Sleep or use a sleep shortcut | Cancel for system sleep; optional display prevention does not override the request. | Automated sleep-stop policy; manual menu/shortcut and assertion check. |
| 4 | Wake after a sleep interruption | Stay inactive and show the stop reason; wake fallback handles a missed pre-sleep notification. | Automated missed-notification and recovery tests; manual wake notification delivery. |
| 5 | Lock the screen or start a screen saver | Continue if the user session remains active and the Mac stays awake; do not bypass authentication. | Manual lock/screen-saver integration check. |
| 6 | Only the display sleeps | Continue the timer; pause cat motion while the display sleeps. | Manual display-only sleep and animation check. |
| 7 | Close, minimize, or hide Cat Run's window | Continue the current timer in the menu bar. | Existing window behavior and lifecycle code; manual window/menu-bar check. |
| 8 | Quit normally | Release assertions and record a quit outcome; reopen inactive. | Automated stop/journal tests; manual normal quit and assertion removal. |
| 9 | Shutdown, restart, or an OS-update restart | Stop on the lifecycle notification or process termination; relaunch inactive. Use the combined logout/restart/shutdown reason when macOS does not identify which. Abrupt termination without a final record becomes an interruption with a separately recorded detection time. | Automated known-stop/recovery tests; manual restart and real login launch. |
| 10 | Log out | End the timer and relaunch inactive. Use the observed lifecycle reason; an unrecorded termination has unknown stop time. | Automated persisted-stop/recovery tests; manual logout/login. |
| 11 | Switch users without logging out | Stop for user switch; returning does not resume the timer. | Generic stop/journal policy tests; manual fast-user-switch notification check. |
| 12 | Begin logout/shutdown, then cancel | Once Cat Run received the stop/termination event, stay inactive or closed. If cancellation preceded any event, an otherwise healthy timer may continue. | Stop/relaunch policy tests; manual cancellation ordering. |
| 13 | App crashes or is force-quit | macOS removes process-owned assertions. On relaunch, recover the unfinished marker as unexpectedly interrupted with unknown cause and stop time; record detection on reopening. | Automated journal recovery and installed-app forced-termination/assertion cleanup; genuine crash testing remains separate. |
| 14 | Mac crashes, loses power, or is forcibly switched off | No execution guarantee during the failure. Relaunch inactive; recover interruption if the marker survived. Never invent an exact end time. | Automated incomplete-record recovery; genuine system failure not simulated by policy tests. |
| 15 | App freezes while its process remains alive | Independent charge checks continue. Missing UI heartbeat stops the session when detected; if the guard stalls too, its last OS lease is at most 45 seconds. | Automated guard tests and an isolated native blocked-main-thread/assertion check; whole-process suspension remains manual. |
| 16 | Unplug the charger | Continue with valid charge at or above 15%. Reset forecast settling; missing runtime produces no warning. Show a low projection inline if a usable one later arrives. | Automated AC-transition/90%-15-minute forecast regression; manual unplug and inline-notice check. |
| 17 | Battery falls below 15%, including on AC | Stop on detection and remain inactive after charging. Exactly 15% is allowed. | Automated cutoff/AC tests and native assertions with injected low charge; real battery reporting remains manual. |
| 18 | Current battery charge becomes unavailable | Stop on a battery-equipped Mac; no auto-resume when it returns. Missing runtime alone does not stop the timer. | Automated unavailable-charge/runtime and desktop tests; manual transient hardware reporting. |
| 19 | Change the clock or timezone | Keep the countdown duration; display saved outcomes in the current local timezone. A clock validity failure ends the session safely. | Automated monotonic-clock, timezone, daylight-saving, locale, and persisted-date tests. |
| 20 | Internet disconnects | Continue normally; the app uses no network service. | Source/entitlement review; optional manual offline check. |
| 21 | Open Cat Run again while already running | Open the existing instance; create no independent second timer. | Automated instance-selection tests; manual double-launch check. |
| 22 | Reach the natural deadline | Release assertions and retain “Timer stopped · Time elapsed” with its local stop date/time across relaunch; log the completion. macOS then applies normal sleep policy. A later charge reading cannot rewrite completion as a battery stop. | Automated deadline ordering, late callback, persistence, and layout tests; manual timed expiry/assertion check. |

See [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md) for the exact completed checks, release evidence, and remaining hardware validation. No automated matrix can establish that every macOS hardware or failure combination is bug-free.
